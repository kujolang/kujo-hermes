# Square film review

The film leads with native installation and developer benefits. A release-rescue story and a reverse audit-trail story were considered; neither made starting as clear. The paired-logo paper bumpers, black evidence scenes and ten CMD slice glitches preserve the existing visual style. One headline carries each benefit. The ending retains “Trust at the speed of AI” and adds a direct install request and repository link.

The full-width source frames deliberately favor complete context over magnification. Main headlines and the install commands carry the message at phone size; small receipt fields remain supporting evidence. The approval clip is the weakest shot because a wide terminal must fit inside a square. It remains uncropped at the user's request. With a new capture, a narrower real terminal viewport could make that prompt easier to read.

Review used the HyperFrames beat, seam and 360-pixel sheets, plus full-size Eval and install frames. The installation prerequisite line was raised from 26 to 29 pixels after the type-floor warning. A fresh reviewer found no P0 or P1 issues. All ten glitches, including the bumper joins, are intentional. The eleven structural lint warnings concern transition bars kept together in the root timeline so they cross scene boundaries; they are not clipping or playback errors.

Actual installation prompts and setup were recorded. The recorder was corrected to use Hermes's selected Python 3.14 interpreter after its old interpreter could not load a new binary dependency. The failed invocation remains in the raw capture and is excluded from the successful instruction edit. No approval or isolation policy was weakened.

Each named tool is paired with its own native evidence view. PackWrite shows an existing pack; Spec validation is not an implementation test; Eval's two checks are limited; ShipCheck has warnings; the receipt verifies integrity rather than correctness. These distinctions remain in the source screens and documentation.

The final delivery report records duration, dimensions, full decode, frame counts, hashes and audio levels. The decoded subject report compares the finished movie with the source screens at the beginning, middle and end of each body scene. Audio is measured, not listened to by the automated reviewer. Static-sheet approval is not a claim that someone watched or heard the complete movie.

The first encoded mix measured −14.15 LUFS / −1.27 dBTP. The mix was rebuilt with two-pass EBU R128 mastering at −16 LUFS and −2 dBTP, then remuxed without changing any video frames. Both sizes were measured again. Delivery gates require −17.5 to −14.5 LUFS and true peak at or below −1.5 dBTP. The final reports contain the resulting measurements.
