## Would this deserve first place?

**Not on the current film and evidence package alone.** Kujo has a strong implementation and useful breadth, but first place requires more than a credible adapter. Chronos has an immediate recovery story; Night Shift demonstrates a complete operational cycle; Precedent Log has a memorable cross-session governance idea. Warden makes overlapping evidence claims, so “accountability” is not a unique differentiator by itself.

The new checkout reproduction materially improves our evidence: a real defect, a specific failure, a small correction, unchanged checks and inspectable before/after receipts. It does not automatically make the old video competitive, and it does not establish that a free model will complete the task reliably. We should compete on that concrete developer outcome and the quality of its execution, not claim we have already won.

## Three actions before November 11

1. **Record the checkout correction inside real Hermes.** Put the actual failing assertion at the center, show a human approval and the same tests passing. End on the two receipts and installation command. Keep the finished film under 90 seconds; do not label the deterministic fixture as an autonomous model run.
2. **Finish the release-candidate proof.** Validate the exact pin, complete dependency preparation and clean install, and retain current-host plus minimum-host CI evidence. A passing validator exit is insufficient when the log contains dependency errors.
3. **Submit one consistent, defensible package.** Match catalog pin, README, film, limitations and X text; get owner authorization and send the catalog PR/video links through the official form before the verified deadline. Recheck organizer rules and form fields directly.

Do not add a dashboard, more integrations or broad release blocking before these are done. The product should remain useful after the contest; a reliable correction-and-evidence workflow serves that goal better than a rushed signature feature.
